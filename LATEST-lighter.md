## 2. Wyclef Jean's Kingston reggae album lands December 11
`Jamaica` · Covered by 1 outlet

Wyclef Jean and producer Tom "Panic" Jones wrote and recorded 'One Night in Kingston' in less than a week with live Jamaican musicians, then spent almost three years picking the right moment to release it. Wyclef says he wants to bring back reggae with a message, and he calls Jones "my Chris Blackwell," a nod to the Island Records founder who helped take Bob Marley global.

**Key facts**
- Seven tracks plus a bonus track, recorded at The Compound in Kingston
- Features Jada Kingdom, Luciano, Stonebwoy and Lila Iké
- It's the reggae part of Wyclef's seven-album 'Quantum Leap' project
- Wyclef and Jones have been friends since their teens

**Worth knowing:** It's a fun story of a long friendship turning into music, and one to add to your December listening.

## 3. Jamaican senator calls for more government accountability
`Jamaica` · Covered by 1 outlet

Opposition Senator Kisha Anderson told the Senate during the State of the Nation Debate that the Government practices accountability "only when it is convenient." She pointed to Jamaica's own track record: since 2013, IMF targets, fiscal rules and a private sector-led oversight committee helped cut public debt from 147% of GDP.

**Key facts**
- Remarks made Friday in the 2026/27 State of the Nation Debate
- Public debt stood at 147% of GDP in 2013
- 2014 fiscal rules set a target of 60% of GDP by 2025/26
- Anderson credited the Economic Programme Oversight Committee (EPOC) with boosting transparency

**Worth knowing:** It's a reminder that Jamaica has turned its finances around before, and that keeping track of how public money is spent is part of that.

## 6. Should dancehall get its own Grammy?
`Jamaica` `Hip Hop & Celebrity` · Covered by 1 outlet

Notable dancehall albums from Vybz Kartel, Beenie Man, Masicka and others were left out of this year's Best Reggae Album nominations, and that has revived calls for a separate dancehall category. Some people would rather rename the category 'Reggae/Dancehall,' as Canada's JUNO Awards just did. Others say there aren't yet enough dancehall releases each year to justify a category of its own.

**Key facts**
- Nominees include Buju Banton, Protoje, Runkus, Chronixx, Ziggy Marley and Shaggy
- Reggae submissions rose to 119 this year, up from 73
- Attorney Lloyd Stanbury supports a separate dancehall category
- Ce'Cile says more Jamaican creatives should become Recording Academy voting members

**Good to know**
- Claims that the Grammys are biased against dancehall come from fans on social media and haven't been substantiated
- The report that the reggae category was nearly cut a few years ago is described as an allegation

**Worth knowing:** Reggae submissions jumped this year, and the debate shows how much Jamaican music has grown and branched out.
