## 1. Rep. Massie files impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Hegseth
`US Headlines` · Covered by 5 outlets

Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, introduced eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Monday, the first day the House was back after the midterms. Most of the charges relate to the war with Iran and whether it followed the War Powers Resolution. Other charges cover strikes on alleged drug boats, the capture of Venezuela's president without congressional approval, and actions related to Sen. Mark Kelly. President Trump and Hegseth both rejected the effort, and a House vote could come within days.

**Key facts**
- The resolution lists 8 charges, including 3 tied to the Iran war under the War Powers Resolution.
- Massie tried this in September, but Speaker Mike Johnson called an early recess, so the resolution expired.
- House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said he is a "hard yes"; Trump said "I'll stand by him."
- Massie lost his May primary to Trump-endorsed challenger Ed Gallrein.

**Worth knowing:** At its core, this is a test of how war powers are split between Congress and the president, and it could be the first impeachment of a Cabinet secretary in Trump's second term.

## 2. Recycling plant workers treated after white powder exposure in Lenoir

**Good to know**

**Worth knowing:** Everyone is reported stable, and investigators are working to find out what the powder was.

## 3. Charlotte approves $2.1 billion airport expansion deal
`Charlotte` · Covered by 3 outlets

Charlotte City Council approved a new 10.5-year airline lease at Charlotte Douglas International Airport, which clears the way for about $2.1 billion in projects. Plans include a bigger Concourse C, a passenger tunnel and a renovated international pavilion. Critics, including a consumer group and the SEIU union, wanted a delay over concerns about fares, competition and subcontractor working conditions. Supporters call the airport the city's economic engine.

**Key facts**
- American Airlines' gates will grow from 93 to 109.
- Airlines' share of airport revenue will rise to 40-45%.
- The airport estimates a $40 billion annual economic impact and 184,000 jobs in the Carolinas.
- More than 50 people signed up to speak at public comment, many of them against the deal.

**Worth knowing:** If you fly out of Charlotte, this probably means more direct flights and more construction over the next decade. Some worry it could also mean higher fares.

## 8. Supreme Court lets Big Bend border construction go ahead for now
`US Headlines` · Covered by 2 outlets

The Supreme Court lifted a lower court's pause, so border barrier construction can continue in and around Big Bend National Park in Texas while the lawsuit plays out. The unsigned decision said the federal judge lacked authority to block the work and noted that federal law lets DHS waive environmental rules. The project has drawn objections from people across the political spectrum who worry about the park's landscape, wildlife and tourism.

**Key facts**
- Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only dissent.
- The plans include 30-foot steel barriers, patrol roads and surveillance systems.
- The lawsuit was brought by the Center for Biological Diversity, a local historic-church nonprofit and a landowner.

**Worth knowing:** This decides what happens to a well-loved national park while the larger court case continues.
