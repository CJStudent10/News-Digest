## 1. North Carolina's House delegation swings toward Democrats
`Charlotte` `US Headlines` · Covered by 2 outlets

North Carolina's midterm results surprised a lot of people. Democrats won several U.S. House seats in districts drawn in 2023 to favor Republicans. As of Thursday, the AP had called nine of the state's 14 House seats for Democrats and none for Republicans, and a few races are still too close to call.

**Key facts**
- The delegation went into Tuesday with 10 Republicans and 4 Democrats.
- Four called races flipped from Republican to Democratic control, including NRCC Chairman Rep. Richard Hudson's 9th District, which went to Richard Ojeda.
- Former Gov. Roy Cooper won the open U.S. Senate seat by nearly 20%.
- In the 3rd District, Republican Greg Murphy leads by just 233 votes, and the race is likely headed for a recount.

**Worth knowing:** If you live in North Carolina, your representative in Washington may be changing, and a few races are still being counted.

## 3. Wisconsin's measles outbreak grows; officials urge vaccination
`Health & Medicine` · Covered by 3 outlets

Wisconsin is seeing its biggest measles outbreak in more than 35 years, and health officials report the state's first measles-related death since 1990, an infant. Nearly all cases are in unvaccinated people, and officials are encouraging residents to check their protection.

**Key facts**
- 431 cases in 13 counties as of Nov. 3, up from 335 a week earlier.
- Nine people have been hospitalized.
- All but three of the patients were unvaccinated.
- Most cases are in Grant, Lafayette, Iowa and Vernon counties, and new cases have appeared in Green Lake County.

**Good to know**
- Officials say the outbreak is linked to out-of-state travel, but the exact source is unknown.
- Reported counts may undercount the real spread, because not everyone who gets sick gets tested.

**Worth knowing:** Measles vaccination is the main tool for stopping spread, so it's a good moment to confirm your family's vaccine records.

## 4. Newsom moves to commute all of California's death sentences
`US Headlines` · Covered by 4 outlets

California Gov. Gavin Newsom says he is commuting the sentences of all 548 people on the state's death row to life without parole. Supporters call it moral leadership, and some victims' advocates oppose it. It comes in his final months in office, amid talk of a possible 2028 presidential run.

**Key facts**
- 548 people are affected: 529 men and 19 women.
- California hasn't carried out an execution since 2006, and Newsom paused executions in 2019.
- Axios says it would be the largest mass commutation in U.S. history, cutting the national death-row population by more than a quarter.
- Fellow Democrat Xavier Becerra is projected to succeed Newsom.

**Worth knowing:** It changes how the state's harshest sentences work, and experts say it could save millions in court costs each year.

## 5. DOJ civil rights chief Harmeet Dhillon resigns
`US Headlines` · Covered by 3 outlets

Harmeet Dhillon, who led the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division for about 18 months, has resigned. She is the first senior DOJ official to leave since the midterms. Her tenure brought big changes to the division's priorities and staff.

**Key facts**
- Attorney General Todd Blanche announced the resignation Thursday and praised her service.
- Dhillon said she told President Trump in early October that she planned to return to private practice after the midterms.
- NPR reports roughly 300 career attorneys left the division during her tenure.
- Courts dismissed the division's lawsuits seeking states' voter information.

**Worth knowing:** A new leader could shift how federal civil rights laws are enforced.

## 7. Tech stocks drop sharply after the midterms
`US Headlines` · Covered by 2 outlets

Investors sold off tech and AI stocks after Democrats won Congress, worried about new rules on AI and limits on data-center construction. The Nasdaq fell more than 5%, and chip stocks in Asia slid the next morning. It's unclear whether the drop will continue.

**Key facts**
- The Nasdaq closed down more than 5.5%, losing an estimated $2.5 trillion in market value, per Dow Jones data.
- South Korea's Kospi fell more than 8.5%, triggering a temporary trading halt.
- Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has pledged a moratorium on new data centers.
- Stocks had risen on Wednesday before reversing on Thursday.

**Worth knowing:** Many people hold tech stocks through 401(k)s and pensions, so it may be worth a calm look at your retirement accounts rather than a panic move.
