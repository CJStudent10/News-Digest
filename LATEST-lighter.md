## 1. Flights are mostly back to normal after the FAA ground stop. Here's what you're owed
`Charlotte` · Covered by 4 outlets

Air travel stopped nationwide for about four hours on Friday, Nov. 13, while the FAA dealt with equipment problems, and travelers in Charlotte were still sorting out delays on Monday. Early findings point to a newly installed router at an Arkansas radio facility, plus a radar site, as the likely causes. Bad news for wallets: airlines usually don't have to cover hotels or meals when the cause is outside their control, though you can get a refund for a canceled flight if you choose not to travel.

**Key facts**
- Only the second nationwide ground stop since Sept. 11, 2001, and the longest in 25 years
- More than 12,000 flights were canceled Friday, according to the FAA
- The router at the center of the probe is in El Dorado, Arkansas; the FBI is helping investigate
- AAA estimates nearly 6 million people will travel for Thanksgiving

**Worth knowing:** With holiday travel coming up, it helps to know you're entitled to a refund if your flight is canceled and you decide not to fly.
