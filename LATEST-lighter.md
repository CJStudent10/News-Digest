## 3. Trump order tells contractors to check with White House before answering Congress
`US Headlines` · Covered by 2 outlets

President Trump signed an executive order Friday directing federal contractors and subcontractors to consult the administration about congressional inquiries and to withhold communications the White House considers privileged. Contractors who don't comply could lose their contracts. The move comes ahead of Democrats taking control of Congress in January, when they plan investigations including into government contracts.

**Key facts**
- Order is titled "Protecting the Executive's Privileges and Enhancing Efficiencies in Federal Contracting"
- It applies to all federal contractors and subcontractors, tens of thousands of companies
- Rep. Robert Garcia, top Democrat on House Oversight, called it an attack on Congress's oversight role
- The White House says it protects executive privilege from "congressional overreach"

**Worth knowing:** Executive privilege isn't absolute, so this could set up lengthy legal fights over how much Congress can investigate in the coming term.

## 6. Yes, even your fridge needs cybersecurity
`Jamaica` · Covered by 1 outlet

After hackers remotely raised fridge temperatures at supermarkets across Europe, a Jamaican cybersecurity expert is urging people and businesses to think about all their connected devices, not just computers. Jevon Davis of Guardsman Cyber Intelligence says some attackers just want to cause disruption, not steal money.

**Key facts**
- The European attacks hit over 700 stores, causing millions of euros in losses
- Affected France in October and around seven other European companies in November
- Davis advises keeping an inventory of connected equipment and knowing who manages it

**Worth knowing:** A simple, practical step: know which of your gadgets are online and who can access them.

## 8. Eminem's publishers add 108 examples to AI music lawsuit
`Hip Hop & Celebrity` · Covered by 1 outlet

Eight Mile Style and Martin Affiliated amended their federal lawsuit against AI music platform Suno, adding 108 examples of tracks they say copied lyrics or music from 26 Eminem-related works. They say this challenges Suno's claim that users can't request specific artists or songs.

**Key facts**
- Amended complaint filed November 19 in the Northern District of California
- Original suit filed November 6, covering 269 compositions
- Publishers seek up to $40.35 million in statutory damages
- Eminem himself is not a plaintiff

**Good to know**
- The infringement claims are allegations; nothing has been decided by a court.
- Suno says it makes original music and has never allowed prompts for specific artists or copyrighted songs.

**Worth knowing:** It's one of the cases that could shape how AI music tools are allowed to work.
