## 1. Microsoft Outlook and Teams had a rough Thursday
`Charlotte` · Covered by 3 outlets

A widespread Microsoft outage left many people unable to send or receive email in Outlook, with Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 apps also affected. Microsoft said some servers unexpectedly went offline after failing automated health checks, and it was testing a fix. Charlotte-area workers described error messages and stuck emails, though one said the downtime gave her a chance to catch up on other work.

**Key facts**
- Nearly 130,000 Outlook problem reports hit Downdetector just after 1 p.m. ET on Thursday
- Microsoft said part of its infrastructure was under 'elevated load' and it was rerouting traffic to balance it
- Microsoft said Teams problems in North America had been restored, though some users still reported issues
- Microsoft has not disclosed the root cause or a full restoration timeline

**Good to know**
- The exact cause is unknown. A cybersecurity expert said Microsoft hasn't clearly ruled out malicious activity, but that doesn't mean it was a cyberattack.

**Worth knowing:** If your inbox acted strange Thursday, it wasn't just you, and it's a good nudge to keep a backup way to reach coworkers.

## 3. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns amid affair scandal

**Key facts**

- Senators Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons are among those demanding he brief Congress
- Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO

**Good to know**
- Confirmed: Huang resigned, and he admitted being unfaithful.

- Reported by U.S. media: a second affair with Nvidia executive Kimberly Powell, who reported to him. Nvidia and Huang's representative declined to comment.

## 6. Democrat Cindy Holscher wins the Kansas governor's race
`US Headlines` · Covered by 2 outlets

**Key facts**

- Masterson campaigned on lower property taxes and being pro-agriculture and pro-business
- Both candidates are 57

**Worth knowing:** The result shapes Kansas policy on schools, taxes and cannabis for the next four years.

## 8. Mississippi elects its first Black U.S. senator in about 150 years
`Health & Medicine` · Covered by 2 outlets

Democrat Scott Colom defeated incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in a close race. He'll be Mississippi's third Black senator ever, following Hiram Revels and Blanche Bruce, who served during Reconstruction. Strong turnout from young voters and Black voters was a big factor, and some students at Jackson State University cast their first-ever votes for him.

**Key facts**
- Mississippi last sent a Democrat to the U.S. Senate in 1982
- Hiram Revels (1870–71) was the first Black person to serve in the Senate
- Blanche Bruce (1875–81), born into slavery, was the first Black senator to serve a full term
- Mississippi has the largest share of Black residents of any U.S. state

**Worth knowing:** It's a historic milestone, and first-time voters helped make it happen.
