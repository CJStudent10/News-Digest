## 1. Netanyahu's rival gets first shot at forming Israel's next government
`US Headlines` · Covered by 2 outlets

Israeli President Isaac Herzog has asked opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief who heads the Yashar party, to try to form a new governing coalition after the Oct. 27 election. Netanyahu's Likud won the most seats, but his coalition fell short of a majority. Outlets describe this as the closest Netanyahu has come to losing power since 2021, though deep divisions could still stop a new government from coming together.

**Key facts**
- Eisenkot has 28 days to form a government.
- Netanyahu's coalition won 53 of the 120 seats in parliament.
- Most of Israel's main parties recommended Eisenkot during talks with the president.

**Worth knowing:** It could mean a change at the top for Israel's longest-serving prime minister, though nothing is settled yet.

## 2. Netanyahu pushes 'unity government' as rivals line up support
`Jamaica` · Covered by 1 outlet

Ahead of the president's decision, Netanyahu warned that an opposition coalition relying on Arab parties would harm the country, and he called for a national unity government of Zionist parties. Eisenkot's camp has opened the door to an unusual alliance that could include ultra-Orthodox parties and the moderate Islamist party Raam, with outside support from the Joint List. Yashar replied that Likud is welcome to join a unity government, as long as it is led by Eisenkot.

**Key facts**
- Eisenkot was recommended by factions representing 67 lawmakers, versus 46 for Netanyahu's bloc.
- Raam's leader has recognised Israel as a 'Jewish state'.
- President Herzog has signalled interest in exploring a national unity government.

**Worth knowing:** How the coalition talks go will decide who runs Israel's government next.
