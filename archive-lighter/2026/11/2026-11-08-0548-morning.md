## 1. Young and Latino voters helped power Democrats' midterm wins
`US Headlines` · Covered by 2 outlets

Exit polls suggest Gen Z and Latino voters swung strongly toward Democrats in Tuesday's midterms, helping the party flip seats and win both chambers of Congress. It's a notable reversal from 2024, when both groups moved toward Trump. Young voters said the economy was their top concern.

**Key facts**
- Democrats carried voters under 30 by nearly 50 points, per exit polling; Gen Z was 11% of the total vote.
- Democrat James Talarico won the Texas Senate race by 12 points, and by about 60 points among Gen Z voters.
- CIRCLE estimates 15% of young 2024 Trump voters backed a Democratic House candidate this year.
- Polling firm BSP found 17% attrition among Latinos who backed Trump in 2024.

**Worth knowing:** It shows that neither party can take big voting groups for granted, which shapes how both will campaign from here.

## 2. Trump asks UFC's Dana White to be press secretary
`US Headlines` · Covered by 2 outlets

President Trump posted on Truth Social asking UFC chief Dana White to become White House press secretary, and to bring several UFC fighters along as 'assistants.' White says he'll meet Trump on Monday but hasn't said whether he wants the job. The role has been open since mid-October.

**Key facts**
- The previous press secretary, Katie Zacharia, left after five days in October.
- White already runs UFC, Power Slap and the new Zuffa Boxing, and sits on Meta's board.
- Trump also named Stephen Miller to replace Susie Wiles as chief of staff.

**Good to know**
- Confirmed: Trump made the offer publicly, and White says a meeting is set for Monday.
- Unclear: whether the offer is fully serious, and whether White's 'On to the next chapter' Instagram post was a joke.
- Reported by CNN, citing an unnamed source: White has declined the idea.

**Worth knowing:** The press secretary is the White House's main daily voice to the public, so who takes the podium matters.

## 4. Only about 1 in 8 Jamaican workers has a private pension
`Jamaica` · Covered by 1 outlet

The Pension Industry Association of Jamaica says private pension coverage is low and is encouraging people of every age to plan for retirement more deliberately. It also notes that retirement is changing, with many people choosing to keep working part-time, consult or start businesses later in life.

**Key facts**
- Private pension coverage was 12.08% of the employed workforce as of September 2025.
- Jamaica's employed labour force is about 1.44 million people.
- The group suggests baby boomers take stock of expenses, savings, debts and expected healthcare costs.

**Worth knowing:** It's a friendly nudge that starting early, even small, makes retirement much easier.

## 5. Jamaica's workplace safety bill to return to Parliament
`Jamaica` · Covered by 1 outlet

Jamaica's Occupational Safety and Health Bill, first tabled in 2017, has been stuck in reviews for nearly a decade, leaving workers covered by older laws. Labour Minister Pearnel Charles Jr says it will come back to the House before the end of the fiscal year. The renewed attention follows several fatal construction accidents this fall.

**Key facts**
- The OSH Bill was first tabled in Parliament in 2017.
- The government is Jamaica's largest employer, with more than 100,000 direct employees.
- The minister made the commitment at a Jamaicans for Justice handbook launch.

**Worth knowing:** Once passed, the bill would set modern safety rules for workplaces across the island.
