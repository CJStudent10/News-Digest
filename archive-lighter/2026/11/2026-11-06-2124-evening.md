## 1. Mississippi's Senate race is still too close to call
`US Headlines` · Covered by 6 outlets

Democrat Scott Colom leads Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 2,500 votes, but thousands of ballots are still being counted and no winner has been declared. Officials can't say exactly how many ballots remain, because Mississippi's 82 counties run their own elections and there are many provisional and late-arriving mail ballots. Both campaigns have legal teams ready.

**Key facts**
- Colom leads by about 2,500 votes as of Friday night
- Estimates of outstanding ballots range from about 15,000 provisional ballots (Colom campaign) to roughly 20,000 total (state GOP chair)
- Voters have until Nov. 10 to resolve provisional ballots; certification deadline is Nov. 13
- Mississippi has no provision for a vote recount

**Worth knowing:** It's a closely watched Senate seat, and the slow count is drawing attention to how Mississippi runs its elections and maintains its voter rolls.

## 2. Stephen Miller named White House chief of staff
`US Headlines` · Covered by 5 outlets

President Trump announced Friday that longtime adviser Stephen Miller will replace Susie Wiles as chief of staff, starting Monday. The move follows Republicans losing control of Congress in Tuesday's midterms. Miller is best known for shaping the administration's immigration enforcement policies. Wiles, the first woman to hold the job, will keep working for Trump on political operations.

**Key facts**
- Miller takes over on Monday; he was previously deputy chief of staff
- Wiles submitted her resignation Thursday night; sources told Axios it was a mutual decision
- Democratic leaders Hakeem Jeffries and Chuck Schumer criticized the pick
- Separately, a federal judge paused construction of Trump's planned arch near the Lincoln Memorial for 14 days

**Worth knowing:** The chief of staff controls access to the president and negotiates with Congress, so this choice signals how the White House may approach a Democratic-led Congress.

## 3. Tesla to wind down Cybertruck production in Texas
`Hip Hop & Celebrity` · Covered by 3 outlets

Elon Musk said Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at its Texas factory around mid-2027 to make room for more Cybercab robotaxis. The truck has had disappointing sales, recalls and quality issues since its 2023 launch. Musk didn't say whether production will move elsewhere or stop entirely.

**Key facts**
- Wind-down planned around the middle of 2027
- The Cybertruck is only built at Tesla's Austin, Texas factory
- The space will go to Cybercab production

**Good to know**
- Confirmed: production is winding down in Texas. Unclear: whether the Cybertruck will be built somewhere else or discontinued.

**Worth knowing:** It's a sign of Tesla shifting its focus toward self-driving robotaxis.

## 6. EU tells TikTok to change its For You feed

The European Union says TikTok's recommendation algorithm can pull users, especially kids, into 'rabbit holes' of harmful content, breaking its Digital Services Act. **Good to know**

**Worth knowing:** It could change how one of the world's most popular apps decides what people see.
