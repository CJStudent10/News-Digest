## 2. Supreme Court weighs who gets to hand out federal fines
`US Headlines` · Covered by 3 outlets

The justices heard arguments over whether the Labor Department can fine a New Jersey farm, Sun Valley Orchards, through its own internal process instead of a jury trial. The department fined the farm about half a million dollars over alleged poor conditions for visa workers, which the farm disputes. The justices seemed divided. The government warned that a ruling against it could disrupt enforcement in programs like Medicare and federal student loans.

**Key facts**
- The case involves the H-2A farmworker visa program
- The farm was fined about $500,000; it disputes the charges
- Government lawyer Sarah Harris called requiring courts for all civil penalties "the nuclear option"
- A decision is expected by the end of June 2027

**Worth knowing:** The ruling could change how many federal agencies enforce rules on businesses.

## 3. Atrium Health on the hook for Briar Creek diesel cleanup
`Charlotte` · Covered by 3 outlets

Diesel fuel from a storage tank at Atrium Health's Mercy Hospital in Charlotte leaked over the weekend, and some of it reached Briar Creek. Mecklenburg County says Atrium must pay for the cleanup and will receive a notice of violation. Any fines will be decided once the full scope of the spill is known.

**Key facts**
- About 2,000 gallons leaked from the tank, according to Storm Water Services
- At least 800 gallons are estimated to have reached the creek
- The EPA is monitoring the cleanup
- Residents are asked to keep themselves and their pets away from the creek until the color and smell are gone

**Worth knowing:** If you or your dog walk near Briar Creek, skip the water for now.

## 8. FDA adds rare eye-risk warning to Ozempic, Zepbound and similar drugs
`Health & Medicine` · Covered by 2 outlets

The FDA is requiring 12 GLP-1 drugs to add a warning about NAION, a rare eye condition that can cause sudden vision loss, usually in one eye. The agency's review found the risk is higher for people on these drugs, but still very low overall. Lilly said the communication isn't specific to its medicines and that it will respond promptly.

**Key facts**
- Drugs affected include Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound and Foundayo
- In a pooled look at 126 trials, NAION occurred at 6.4 per 100,000 patient-years on GLP-1s vs 3.8 on placebo or other treatments
- The review was prompted partly by a 2025 JAMA Ophthalmology study
- The FDA announced the change on November 9

**Good to know**
- These drugs are already FDA approved; this is a label update, not a recall
- Animal data showed no clear mechanism, and NAION risk factors such as diabetes and sleep apnea overlap with conditions common in GLP-1 patients, which makes cause and effect hard to pin down
- If you take one of these drugs, talk to your doctor before making changes, and get prompt care for any sudden vision changes

**Worth knowing:** It's useful to know if you or someone close to you uses these popular medicines.
