## 1. Trump Offers UFC's Dana White the Press Secretary Job
`US Headlines` · Covered by 3 outlets

President Trump posted on Saturday that he has offered the White House press secretary role to UFC chief Dana White, a longtime friend. White, speaking at a boxing event in London, said he'd fly to Washington for a Monday meeting but didn't say yes. The offer comes during a broader White House shake-up after the midterms.

**Key facts**
- White plans to meet Trump at the White House on Monday.
- Trump suggested White bring UFC figures like Khabib Nurmagomedov and Daniel Cormier as assistants.
- Katie Zacharia left the press secretary post after five days; Karoline Leavitt departed in August.
- Trump named Stephen Miller to replace Susie Wiles as chief of staff.

**Good to know**
- Confirmed: Trump made the offer publicly, and White confirmed the Monday meeting.
- Unclear: Whether White is seriously considering the job. Asked about his workload, he said, "Yeah, I got a lot of jobs already."

**Worth knowing:** The press secretary is the president's main voice to reporters, so who fills it shapes how the White House communicates.

## 2. House May Vote on Impeaching Defense Secretary Hegseth This Week
`US Headlines` · Covered by 4 outlets

Republican Rep. Thomas Massie plans to introduce an impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, possibly as early as Monday. Likely incoming Speaker Hakeem Jeffries says he's a "hard yes," and Axios reports GOP leaders expect enough Republican defections for it to pass. Representative-elect Nancy Lacore, a retired admiral whom Hegseth removed in 2025, also weighed in.

**Key facts**
- Massie's resolution has eight articles, including an alleged violation of the 1973 War Powers Resolution over the Iran war.
- Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in last week's midterms; the new Congress starts in January.
- Lacore flipped South Carolina's 1st District and said she was never given a reason for her removal.
- Jeffries said House Democrats are not currently focused on impeaching President Trump.

**Worth knowing:** This is an early test of how the newly reshaped Congress will act as a check on the administration.
