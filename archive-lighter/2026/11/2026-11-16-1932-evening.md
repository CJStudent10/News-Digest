## 1. Charlotte travelers still sorting out the ground-stop aftermath
`Charlotte` · Covered by 5 outlets

Charlotte Douglas was still feeling the ripple effects on Monday from Friday's nationwide FAA ground stop, with some travelers rebooked several times and luggage scattered. Investigators are looking at a newly installed router at a radio facility in El Dorado, Arkansas, as one of two likely causes. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the failure may have started during an upgrade of old equipment, and the FBI is helping look into it.

**Key facts**
- It was only the second nationwide ground stop since Sept. 11, 2001, and the longest in 25 years.
- The FAA said more than 12,000 flights were canceled Friday; other outlets report different totals.
- FlightAware showed 800+ delays and 270+ cancellations nationwide by Monday afternoon.
- FAA administrator Bryan Bedford says affected passengers should be rebooked or get a full refund.

**Worth knowing:** If your flight was affected, the FAA says you're owed either a new flight or a full refund.

## 2. United hits a brief tech snag, then fixes it
`Charlotte` · Covered by 4 outlets

United Airlines had a 'widespread system outage' Monday that held departing flights at the gate for roughly an hour to 90 minutes before it was resolved. Some pilots said it affected weight-and-balance calculations. The outage came the same day a JPMorgan Chase software bug caused online banking and Zelle delays.

**Key facts**
- United said flights already in the air and United Express flights were not affected.
- United said there's no sign it was a cybersecurity issue; Chase also said its bug wasn't cyber-related.
- Only one Charlotte flight, to Denver, was affected.
- Charlotte had 310 delays and about 30 cancellations Monday, close to a normal day.

**Good to know**
- The cause of United's outage hasn't been explained yet.

**Worth knowing:** It was a quick fix, and Charlotte travel was largely back to normal by Monday.

## 3. Charlotte schools take it slow on picking a new superintendent
`Charlotte` · Covered by 3 outlets

The Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools search committee is planning a longer, more community-focused search, with a decision possibly coming in June 2027. Parents, students, staff and residents will get an online survey about what they want in a leader, plus spring town halls. Committee chair Anna London said the district needs to show it actually uses the feedback.

- Proposed steps: search firm picked in February, applications open in March, semifinalists May 24.

**Worth knowing:** If you're in the district, you'll get a real chance to weigh in on who leads Charlotte's schools.

## 4. U.S. flights nearly back to normal after the big ground stop
`Jamaica` · Covered by 2 outlets

Air travel across the U.S. was close to normal on Monday after Friday's four-hour nationwide halt on takeoffs. Officials blamed aging equipment that became overloaded, and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy is now asking Congress for $50 billion to modernize air traffic control. President Trump blamed old equipment and said he doesn't take responsibility, calling on Congress to fund upgrades.

**Key facts**
- The halt was lifted at 8:15 p.m. Friday after about four hours.
- The Wall Street Journal reported old networking gear in Arkansas, and possibly Texas, overloaded and affected 17 of 22 major air control centers.
- Total cancellations topped 17,000 by Sunday, per Bloomberg.
- The FAA said some radio and radar feeds stayed working, so controllers could still track planes.

**Worth knowing:** How Congress responds to the funding request could shape air travel for years, and airlines say they're ready for the holiday rush.

## 5. Two arrested in $196K email scam aimed at Ashe County hospital
`Charlotte` · Covered by 3 outlets

Ashe Memorial Hospital lost more than $196,000 after scammers posing as vendors emailed staff and asked them to change payment details. Investigators traced the money through banks in several states, and two men have been arrested and brought to North Carolina. A third person has been charged and is still being sought.

**Key facts**
- Themba Machila was arrested in Georgia and extradited Oct. 21.
- Richard Folarin Eniola Shodolamu of Yonkers, N.Y., was extradited Nov. 9.
- The SBI and West Jefferson Police are leading the case and expect more arrests.

**Good to know**
- These are charges and allegations; the suspects have not been convicted.

**Worth knowing:** It's a good reminder: always double-check any email asking to change where payments go.

## 6. U.S. expected to lose its 'measles eliminated' status
`Health & Medicine` `Jamaica` · Covered by 2 outlets

The U.S. is expected to lose its measles-elimination designation this week, which it earned in 2000, as cases keep spreading. 'Eliminated' doesn't mean zero cases; it means outbreaks end within a year, and that's no longer happening. Health experts point to falling vaccination rates.

**Key facts**
- 5,182 confirmed U.S. cases so far in 2026, the most since 1991, per the CDC.
- Almost all of this year's cases were in unvaccinated people.
- A Pan American Health Organization expert panel made the recommendation; a news conference is set for Tuesday.

**Good to know**
- The panel's ruling was reported through anonymous sources; the PAHO director still has to make the final call.
- HHS did not respond to a request for comment.

**Worth knowing:** The measles vaccine remains the main protection, so it's a good time to check that your family's shots are current.
