## 1. Trump publicly offers White House press secretary job to UFC chief Dana White
`US Headlines` · Covered by 10 outlets (L 4 · C 3 · R 3)

President Trump said on Truth Social that he had offered the vacant White House press secretary job to UFC CEO Dana White, and asked him to bring six UFC fighters as assistants to 'defend us against the Lunatic Left.' White posted an image of himself at the briefing room podium captioned 'On to the next chapter' and said he would meet Trump in Washington on Monday, adding, 'I got a lot of jobs already.' CNN reported, citing a source, that White had declined, and The Guardian said CNN also quoted an aide calling the offer a joke. The post comes after Republicans lost both chambers of Congress and after Trump named Stephen Miller to replace Susie Wiles as chief of staff.

**Key facts**
- The press secretary post has been without a permanent holder since Karoline Leavitt left in August; her successor, Katie Zacharia, left after about five days in October.
- White said he has a meeting with Trump on Monday.
- White House communications director Steven Cheung posted: 'Hell yeah! Do it, @DanaWhite!'
- Fighters named in Trump's post include Islam Makhachev, Khabib Nurmagomedov, Justin Gaethje, Daniel Cormier and Amanda Nunes.
- Trump announced Friday that Stephen Miller will replace Susie Wiles as chief of staff.

**How it's being framed**
- **Left:** The Guardian presented the offer as possibly a stunt to change the subject from the party's election losses. It noted White's estimated $600m+ fortune against the job's $195,000 salary and Trump's misspelling of Kayla Harrison's name. It also reported that Nurmagomedov is said to be close to Putin, and contrasted conservative criticism of a Democrat's use of 'Alhamdulillah' with Makhachev's use of the same phrase. PBS carried the AP's straight account, stressing that no one at the White House confirmed the offer.
- **Center:** The BBC and AP (via the Korea Herald) treated it as an unconventional, unconfirmed announcement set against a White House in transition after the midterms. They covered the Miller appointment and White's combative history with journalists, including a 2009 tirade that used an anti-gay slur.
- **Right:** Fox News focused on White's confirmation of Monday's meeting and on Cheung's endorsement. It traced the long Trump–White friendship and White's role in connecting Trump with young online audiences, and looked ahead to Democratic oversight plans, including scrutiny of Miller. The Washington Examiner briefly relayed White's comments.

**Disputed or unclear**
- Whether the offer is genuine: CNN reported White declined and an aide called it a joke, while White's posts and planned meeting leave it open.
- Whether White's Instagram post was tongue-in-cheek.

**Why it matters:** Who speaks for the White House shapes how the administration deals with the press as it faces a Democratic Congress.

## 3. Israel's president gives opposition leader Eisenkot first chance to form government
`US Headlines` · Covered by 6 outlets (L 0 · C 4 · R 2)

President Isaac Herzog said he will task Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the opposition Yashar party, with forming a government after the inconclusive Oct. 27 election. Eisenkot was recommended by lawmakers representing 67 seats, against 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. However, the 67 include Arab-led parties that most of Eisenkot's prospective partners have refused to rely on. Eisenkot will have 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension, starting Sunday evening. He has called for a 'national emergency government,' while Netanyahu has warned that a coalition with Arab parties would 'dismantle the State of Israel from within.'

**Key facts**
- Netanyahu's bloc won 53 of 120 seats; Eisenkot's four-party bloc won 48.
- Arab-led parties won an unprecedented 19 seats.
- Likud is the largest single party, with 27 seats.
- United Torah Judaism's seven members endorsed neither candidate.

**How it's being framed**
- **Center:** The BBC, AFP (via the Korea Herald), Axios and The Hill described it as a major setback for Netanyahu but stressed the hard arithmetic. They laid out the possible routes: unity government, Arab-backed coalition, or ultra-Orthodox deals. AFP added Netanyahu's corruption charges and the ICC warrant against him.
- **Right:** Fox News also called it a possible step toward ending Netanyahu's tenure, but emphasised that 67 endorsements are not a 67-seat coalition. It detailed Eisenkot's four principles, including treating Hamas as a terrorist group to be dismantled. The Washington Examiner briefly called it a setback for Netanyahu.

**Disputed or unclear**
- Whether Eisenkot can build a majority without Arab parties or ultra-Orthodox support.
- Whether a unity government will emerge, and whether Netanyahu would be part of it.
- Avigdor Liberman's position, which reportedly shifted on Friday.

**Why it matters:** This is the closest Netanyahu has come to losing power since 2021, with consequences for Israel's wars and diplomacy.

## 4. Magnitude 5.7 earthquake injures more than 20 in central Japan
`Asia Business & Politics` · Covered by 3 outlets (L 1 · C 1 · R 1)

A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck Shizuoka Prefecture at 1:04 a.m. Sunday, according to the Japan Meteorological Agency. It shattered store windows and disrupted trains, including the Shinkansen. More than 20 people were injured in Aichi and Shizuoka, mostly with minor injuries, and no deaths or tsunami warning were reported. The agency warned of possible strong aftershocks over the coming week and said no change had been detected in the Nankai Trough.

**Key facts**
- The quake was about 40km (25 miles) deep in Shizuoka Prefecture.
- The USGS measured it at magnitude 5.5.
- Local reports cite at least 22 to 24 injured.
- A separate magnitude 4.3 quake hit Kumamoto later Sunday.

**How it's being framed**
- **Left:** Al Jazeera gave a factual account focused on injuries, aftershock warnings and the Nankai Trough.
- **Center:** CNA reported regional injury counts and noted an Osaka resident who was hospitalised. It placed the quake against Japan's seismic history, including a deadly July quake and the 2011 disaster.
- **Right:** The New York Post carried AP-style reporting similar to the others, adding the USGS magnitude and Japan's preparedness advice.

**Disputed or unclear**
- Injury counts vary between 16 and 24 depending on the source.
- Magnitude estimates differ: 5.7 from Japan's agency, 5.5 from the USGS.

**Why it matters:** Aftershock warnings and concern about a Nankai megaquake keep risk elevated for a densely populated region.

## 5. France sees fourth straight weekend of cost-of-living protests
`Korea` · Covered by 3 outlets (L 1 · C 2 · R 0)

France's Interior Ministry counted 146,000 protesters nationwide on Saturday, including 46,000 in Paris; unions put the figures at 250,000 and 75,000. Turnout was down from previous weeks. Police used tear gas in Paris and Lyon, made at least 360 arrests, and blocked a banned Yellow Vests rally near the Elysee Palace, reportedly arresting two of its leaders. The movement began with high school students in September and has broadened to wages and living costs. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu meets unions and employers on Monday.

**Key facts**
- About 70,000 security personnel were deployed.
- The Interior Ministry counted 575,000 protesters at union-led demonstrations on Nov. 5.

- France holds presidential elections in spring.

**How it's being framed**

**Disputed or unclear**

- Allegations of police brutality are under dozens of internal investigations.

**Why it matters:** The unrest is putting pressure on France's budget and could shape a presidential race in which the far right sees its best chance yet.
