## 2. Israel's president asks opposition leader Eisenkot to try to form a government
`US Headlines` · Covered by 10 outlets (L 4 · C 4 · R 2)

Israeli President Isaac Herzog said on Saturday he would task Gadi Eisenkot, the former military chief who leads the opposition Yashar party, with forming a government after the October 27 election. Neither Netanyahu's bloc nor Eisenkot's won a majority. Most parties, including two Arab-led lists, recommended Eisenkot. He will receive the mandate formally on Sunday and has 28 days to build a 61-seat majority, with a possible 14-day extension. Netanyahu called for a "national unity government" and warned against relying on what he called "terrorist supporters," his term for the Arab-led parties.

**Key facts**
- Netanyahu's Likud won 27 seats, his bloc 53 of 120; Eisenkot's four-party bloc won 48.
- Factions with 67 lawmakers recommended Eisenkot and 46 backed Netanyahu; United Torah Judaism's 7 members endorsed no one.
- The Arab-led Joint List and Ra'am together hold 19 seats and recommended Eisenkot.
- Eisenkot on X: "Now is the time to form a national emergency government."
- If no one forms a government, Israel faces new elections; it held five between 2019 and 2022.

**How it's being framed**
- **Left:** PBS (AP) and The Guardian focused on the Arab-led parties as fraught potential kingmakers. AP described as unsubstantiated Netanyahu's claims that Arab parties "stole" the election. The Guardian profiled Eisenkot as "no peacenik": he opposes a Palestinian state and devised the "Dahiya doctrine," but has also vowed to crack down on violent West Bank settlers.
- **Center:** Axios and The Hill gave brief accounts. Axios called it the closest Netanyahu has come to losing power since 2021 and noted that deep divisions could still block a government.
- **Right:** Fox News detailed the seat arithmetic and stressed that 67 endorsements do not equal a 67-seat coalition, since some partners rule out relying on Arab parties. It quoted Eisenkot's four founding principles at length, including dismantling Hamas. The Washington Examiner briefly called it a setback for Netanyahu.

**Disputed or unclear**
- Whether Eisenkot's bloc will accept support from Arab-led parties, formally or from outside the coalition.
- Whether a broad unity government with Likud is possible, given disputes over ultra-Orthodox draft exemptions and an October 7 inquiry.

**Why it matters:** The outcome could end Netanyahu's long tenure and reshape Israel's approach to Gaza, the West Bank and its domestic divisions.

## 3. Gunman kills six in Krakow's main square; Polish leaders call it terrorism
· Covered by 8 outlets (L 4 · C 2 · R 2)

A 25-year-old Polish man drove through Krakow's Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon and fired an automatic weapon at random people from his car. He killed six people and wounded more than a dozen before police shot and arrested him within about three minutes. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the suspect was "fascinated by far-right ideologies" and held "ultranationalistic" views, and Prime Minister Donald Tusk called the attack terrorism. Police later found the bodies of two women and a man, likely relatives, at the suspect's home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski. Poland will observe two days of national mourning on Sunday and Monday.

**Key facts**
- The dead were four women and two men: three Polish women, one British national, and two not yet identified.
- Five foreigners were among the injured, from Austria, Britain, Germany, Portugal and Russia; two police officers were seriously wounded.
- The suspect wore military-style fatigues and a bulletproof vest, and more weapons and ammunition were found in his car.
- Authorities said he appears to have acted alone and was not a soldier; his name has not been released.
- Krakow's mayor declared city mourning until Wednesday, saying "The square will belong to us again."

**How it's being framed**
- **Left:** The Guardian ran live updates stressing the far-right motive, police heroism and Tusk's warning about online disinformation. PBS carried the AP report, which linked to a piece on hostility toward Ukrainian refugees in Poland.
- **Center:** Deutsche Welle and the BBC gave factual explainers. They covered casualties, the suspect's views and international condolences. The BBC added verified footage, the injured officers' names, and Polish media reports that the suspect had a gun permit.
- **Right:** The New York Post ran the AP report under a headline calling the gunman a "lunatic" while keeping the far-right detail. Fox News emphasised the tourist setting, Tusk's appeal to politicians not to exploit the tragedy, and police denials of false rumours. It linked to other recent attacks, including fears of copycat school attacks in Poland.

**Disputed or unclear**
- The injured count varies: 16 (DW, BBC, including the attacker) versus 15 (AP, Fox).
- Whether the guns were legally held; Polish media reported he had a permit and may have taken weapons from his family home.
- The full motive and the circumstances of the deaths at his home are under investigation.
- AP attributes the "act of terror" description to the defence minister; other outlets quote Tusk calling it terrorism.

**Why it matters:** Mass shootings are rare in Poland, and an apparently ideologically motivated attack is likely to shape debate over extremism and gun ownership there.
