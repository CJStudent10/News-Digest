## 1. Apple reports record September quarter as foldable iPhone Duo stays in short supply
`Korea` · Covered by 5 outlets (L 1 · C 3 · R 1)

Apple reported its first quarterly results under new CEO John Ternus, who succeeded Tim Cook in September. Revenue rose 12% to about $114.5bn, and iPhone revenue grew 17% to about $57.6bn. The company said its new foldable iPhone Duo, priced near $2,000, is supply-constrained, and it could not predict when supply will meet demand. Ternus described memory-chip price increases tied to the AI data-centre boom as 'unprecedented', and Apple has raised prices across its products. In South Korea, sales of Samsung's rival Galaxy Z Fold8 rose about 20% in the week after the Duo launched, though a causal link has not been shown.

**Key facts**
- Quarterly revenue was $114.5bn, up 12%. iPhone revenue was about $57.6bn, up 17%.
- Mac revenue was about $10.8bn. Outlets reported growth of 24% (The Verge, Fox Business) or 27% (BBC).
- iPad revenue fell 10%. The Duo's sales are not included in this quarter.
- Apple acknowledged on Oct. 26 that a 'small number' of Duo screens cracked along the fold and offered replacements.
- Galaxy Z Fold8 sales in Korea rose more than 20% in the week of Oct. 23-29 compared with the prior week, according to telecom industry figures.

**How it's being framed**
- **Left:** The Verge focused on the iPhone revenue record despite there being no new standard model. It linked the growth to pricier Pro models and price increases, and noted other product launches such as a touchscreen MacBook Pro.
- **Center:** The BBC highlighted the Duo shortage and its screen problems, noting executives did not mention the defects on the earnings call, and set this against memory-cost pressures and price rises. Korean outlets focused on Samsung's sales gain. The Korea Herald stressed that there is no evidence linking the gain to Apple's problems, while Yonhap cited industry sources crediting Samsung's durability and AI features.
- **Right:** Fox Business framed the results as a record quarter and best-ever fiscal year under the new CEO, beating Wall Street estimates. It quoted Ternus at length on supply, memory costs and the Mac's 'AI moment', and noted analyst doubts about whether the iPhone 18 can match the iPhone 17's upgrade cycle.

**Disputed or unclear**
- The cause of the Duo screen cracking has not been established, and the scale of returns is unclear. Ternus said 'a very small percentage' of units were returned.
- Whether Samsung's Korean sales gain reflects buyers switching from Apple is unconfirmed.
- Outlets reported different Mac growth figures: 24% versus 27%.

**Why it matters:** The results show how AI-driven chip and memory costs are pushing up prices for consumer electronics.

## 2. Democrat Haley Stevens defends endorsing Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan Senate race
`US Headlines` · Covered by 6 outlets (L 2 · C 1 · R 3)

Rep. Haley Stevens, who lost the Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, endorsed Republican Mike Rogers two days before the election. The endorsement followed a dispute over an outside group's ad that blamed the cost of living on US support for Israel; El-Sayed's campaign then disinvited her. Democratic leaders including Chuck Schumer and Nancy Pelosi condemned the move, Michigan's Democratic Party chair said she is not a party member, and several of her staff resigned. Stevens called it 'a decision of conscience' and said suggestions that her fiancé influenced her were sexist. Vice President JD Vance said the endorsement 'certainly' helps Rogers.

**Key facts**
- Stevens announced her endorsement of Rogers on Sunday, two days before Election Day.
- El-Sayed's campaign said it raised more than $2.4m after the endorsement, including from more than 26,000 first-time donors.
- Schumer called the endorsement 'a despicable betrayal'.
- Stevens' fiancé, Jason Turkish, backed Rogers the week before, citing El-Sayed's stance on Israel.

- **Right:** Fox News led with Vance's comments calling El-Sayed 'a crazy person'. It highlighted El-Sayed's ties to streamer Hasan Piker and Rogers' accusations about 'terrorist sympathizers'. The New York Post and Washington Examiner centred on Stevens' 'sexism' rebuttal and her criticism of the Democratic backlash as intolerance of dissent.

**Disputed or unclear**

## 5. Supreme Court declines Justice Department bid to set aside 20,000 California ballots
`US Headlines` · Covered by 6 outlets (L 1 · C 5 · R 0)

On Monday, hours after the request, the Supreme Court denied a Justice Department application to block California from counting mail ballots from about 20,000 voters the administration says are likely noncitizens. The one-sentence order gave no reasoning and noted no dissents. Lower courts had already rejected the request, and state officials and analysts said the list contained many errors. A separate federal judge barred the Justice Department from threatening election workers.

**Key facts**
- The order was unsigned, with no noted dissents, and California was not asked to respond first.
- More than six million mail ballots had been received in California as of Sunday night.
- A Justice Department lawyer acknowledged the noncitizen lists had a 'high error rate', according to MS NOW as cited by CNBC.
- Judge David Carter wrote that never before had the federal government sought to coerce a state on the eve of an election.

**How it's being framed**
- **Left:** The Guardian called the ruling the latest 'judicial rebuke' of unprecedented federal intervention, and tied it to Trump's low approval ratings and other campaign news.
- **Center:** Outlets broadly framed the ruling as a legal blow to the administration. CNBC quoted California AG Rob Bonta calling the bid a 'Hail Mary' and also laid out the Solicitor General's argument. The BBC and Deutsche Welle stressed errors in the list and research showing voter fraud is rare. Axios noted the administration says it is defending election law.

**Disputed or unclear**
- Whether any of the flagged voters are actually noncitizens. A filed analysis found thousands of apparent US citizens on the list.
- The Justice Department's separate appeal in Alaska is ongoing.

**Why it matters:** The ruling sets limits on federal intervention in how states run and count elections.
