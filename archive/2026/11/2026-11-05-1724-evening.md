## 1. Newsom commutes all 548 California death sentences to life without parole
`US Headlines` · Covered by 9 outlets (L 2 · C 4 · R 3)

California Gov. Gavin Newsom, in his final months in office, said he will convert all 548 death sentences in the state to life in prison without parole. He called state-sanctioned killing incompatible with California's values. Under the state constitution, the California Supreme Court must approve clemency for anyone convicted of more than one felony, which Newsom's office says covers 382 of the cases. The ACLU and anti-death-penalty advocates praised the move. Victims' advocates and some prosecutors criticized it, and a former district attorney told the New York Post that opponents will challenge it in court.

**Key facts**
- 548 people are affected (529 men and 19 women, per The Guardian); 382 commutations need state Supreme Court approval, according to Newsom's office.
- Newsom imposed a moratorium on executions in 2019; California's last execution was in 2006.
- In 2016, 53% of California voters rejected a ballot measure to abolish the death penalty.
- Newsom wrote: "As Governor, I alone bear responsibility for this decision and its impact."
- Axios says that if completed, it would be the largest mass commutation of death sentences in US history, cutting the national death-row population by more than a quarter.

**How it's being framed**
- **Left:** The Guardian and PBS/AP emphasized Newsom's reasoning and the system's problems: long waits for habeas counsel and more than 200 exonerations nationwide. They quoted supporters including the ACLU, Martin Luther King III, Bryan Stevenson and a former prisons director who oversaw executions. The Guardian highlighted Polly Klaas's sister, Jess Nichol, backing the commutations, and linked the move to the failed execution of Christa Pike in Tennessee.
- **Center:** Deutsche Welle and Axios gave straightforward accounts that included both the ACLU's praise and voters' past support for the death penalty. Both noted Newsom's possible 2028 presidential run, and Axios put that angle in its headline.
- **Right:** Fox News and the New York Post centered victims' families and prosecutors. They quoted Polly Klaas's father, Marc Klaas ("He has definitely secured the serial killer vote now"), LA County DA Nathan Hochman on voters twice keeping the death penalty, and former DA Anne Marie Schubert. The Post listed notorious killers who were spared and used charged language such as "maniacs." Fox also included praise from an anti-death-penalty spiritual adviser.

**Disputed or unclear**
- Whether the California Supreme Court will approve the 382 commutations that need its sign-off.
- Whether victims' advocates' promised court challenge will go forward or succeed.
- Polly Klaas's relatives disagree publicly: her sister backs the move and her father opposes it.
- Critics say the move overrides voters, who rejected repeal in 2016 (Hochman says voters kept the penalty in two separate initiatives); Newsom argues the constitution gives governors clemency power for exactly this purpose.

**Why it matters:** It would remove the largest death row in the country and tests how far a governor's clemency power can go against voters' past choices.

## 2. Melania Trump hosts Russian and Ukrainian officials after Oleshky ceasefire
`US Headlines` · Covered by 8 outlets (L 3 · C 4 · R 1)

First lady Melania Trump hosted Russian and Ukrainian officials at the White House to mark a six-day ceasefire in the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky in Kherson region, which both sides credit her with brokering. Kremlin envoy Kirill Dmitriev and Ukrainian negotiator Davyd Arakhamia praised her, and she called for a more enduring peace and handed them letters for Putin and Zelenskyy. President Trump separately met Dmitriev, declined to describe the discussion, and said a "big agreement" involving more than Ukraine is in the works. Ukraine's general staff says Russia used the pause to fortify positions, and the Kremlin says no further humanitarian missions are planned.

**Key facts**
- The ceasefire in Oleshky and nearby areas of Kherson lasted six days.
- Arakhamia called it the "biggest de-escalation since 2022."

- The scope of Melania Trump's personal role, which the BBC described as key but unspecified.
- What President Trump discussed with Dmitriev and what the promised "big agreement" involves.
