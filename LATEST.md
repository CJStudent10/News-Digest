## 1. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO; Trump says he persuaded the board
`AI & Cybersecurity` `US Headlines` `Asia Business & Politics` · Covered by 17 outlets (L 7 · C 8 · R 2)

Nvidia said it received input from 'senior levels' of the US government and is confident his return poses no national security risk. **Key facts**
- Huang resigned on November 12 and was reinstated on November 23, 2026.

- Interim CEO Colette Kress returns to her role as chief financial officer.

- House chairs Moolenaar, Crawford and Mast said Huang and Tim Teter will appear for transcribed interviews on December 1 and 2.

## 2. Court clears Army to detonate degraded lead azide at Iowa ammunition plant
`US Headlines` · Covered by 10 outlets (L 3 · C 6 · R 1)

A three-judge panel of the 8th US Circuit Court of Appeals ruled Monday that the EPA's emergency permit adequately limits the spread of lead dust. That allows the Army to detonate 68 barrels of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown. Earlier in the day, Trump said the operation should proceed only if the court and Democratic Gov.-elect Rob Sand agreed, and that otherwise Sand would be held responsible for 'any and all death or destruction.' Sand said the decision belongs to the administration and called for more transparency. Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle said the detonation will go ahead Wednesday morning as a 'consensus decision.'

**Key facts**
- The detonation involves 68 barrels of degraded lead azide and was originally set for Friday, November 20.
- The date has changed several times; it is now set for Wednesday, depending on wind conditions.
- The Army has warned that a lead-dust plume could extend up to 100 miles.
- Environmental groups represented by Earthjustice challenged the EPA permit; Earthjustice said it respects the ruling.
- Gov. Kim Reynolds activated the state emergency operations center, and the FAA restricted flights in the area.

**How it's being framed**
- **Left:** The Guardian and PBS highlighted Trump's attempt to put responsibility on Sand, health risks from lead (citing the CDC that no exposure is safe), and residents' complaints about poor communication. PBS noted that Iowa Public Radio reporting complicates Trump's claim that the Obama administration was to blame.
- **Center:** AP (via WBTV), Axios and The Hill focused on the court's reasoning, the safeguards it cited, Trump's 'unusual twist' of seeking the governor-elect's approval, and the state's frustration over transparency.
- **Right:** The Washington Examiner centered Telle's announcement and his description of a 'consensus' among more than ten federal agencies and both governors. It described the lawsuit as an obstacle that had been overcome.

**Disputed or unclear**
- The quantity is described as 'several tons,' 'nearly 9,500lb' and '5 tons' in different outlets.
- Telle says Sand agreed; Sand's own statements say the decision is up to the administration.
- Who is responsible for the deterioration: Trump blames the Obama administration, while PBS cites Iowa Public Radio on a 2004 Army explosives project, and it is unclear why the barrels were not disposed of earlier.

**Why it matters:** Nearby residents across Iowa and Illinois face a potential lead-dust plume, and the episode raises questions about federal transparency and accountability.
